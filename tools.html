AIM Statement Canvas

Define a SMART aim before starting any QI project — required for QIPAT

AIM Statement Canvas · Society for Acute Medicine QI Toolkit
SAM QI Toolkit
💡 SMART AIM formula: "We will improve [WHAT] from [BASELINE] to [TARGET] in [POPULATION] at [SETTING] by [DATE]."
W — What will be improved? (the indicator)
B — Baseline (current performance with source)
T — Target (desired performance, evidence-based)
P — Population / Setting (who, where)
D — Date / Timeframe for achievement
Full AIM Statement (written out)

NHS Making Data Count rules for a signal of improvement on a run chart:
(a) Shift: 6+ consecutive data points all above or all below the median  |  (b) Trend: 5+ consecutive points all going up or all going down  |  (c) Astronomical data point: one unusually high or low point  |  Annotate each PDSA cycle start date on the X-axis with a vertical dashed line.

📋 SAM QI Toolkit · Ref: NHS Making Data Count (NHSE/I) · Aligns with SQUIRE 2.0 Items 11b and 13b

6

Failure Modes & Effects Analysis (FMEA)

Proactively identify risks before implementing your change — essential for patient safety QI
Risk / Safety ▸
FMEA Template · Society for Acute Medicine QI Toolkit
SAM QI Toolkit
Process Step Potential Failure Mode Potential Effect Severity (1–10) Occurrence (1–10) Detection (1–10) RPN (S×O×D) Recommended Action
RPN (Risk Priority Number) = Severity × Occurrence × Detection. Score each 1–10. RPN ≥ 100 = high priority for action. Aim to reduce the highest RPN scores first.

📋 SAM QI Toolkit · FMEA · Aligns with SQUIRE 2.0 Item 12 (Ethical Considerations — harm prevention)

SQUIRE 2.0 Reporting Checklist

Confirm all 18 items are addressed before submitting your QI report for publication
Reporting / Publication ▸
SQUIRE 2.0 Reporting Checklist · Society for Acute Medicine QI Toolkit
SAM QI Toolkit
Title & Abstract (Items 1–2)
1
TitleIndicates manuscript concerns a healthcare improvement initiative
2
AbstractStructured summary: background, local problem, methods, interventions, results, conclusions
Introduction — Why did you start? (Items 3–6)
3
Problem DescriptionNature and significance of the local problem with baseline data
4
Available KnowledgeSummary of what is currently known, including SAMBA/national benchmarks and guidelines
5
RationaleFrameworks/theories used (e.g., Model for Improvement, COM-B, Lean, Driver Diagram)
6
Specific AimsSMART AIM statement — what the project and report aim to achieve
Methods — What did you do? (Items 7–12)
7
ContextAMU type, size, staffing, EPR, culture, competing pressures — key contextual factors
8
InterventionsDescription reproducible by others (8a); team composition and roles (8b)
9
Study of InterventionApproach to assessing impact (9a); attribution approach (9b)
10
MeasuresOutcome, process & balancing measures with operational definitions (10a–c)
11
AnalysisQual/quant methods (11a); time-series analysis — run chart / SPC (11b)
12
Ethical ConsiderationsQI vs research classification; HRA decision tool; data governance; conflicts of interest
Results — What did you find? (Item 13)
13
Results (6 sub-items)PDSA evolution (a) · Process/outcome data as run chart (b) · Contextual interactions (c) · Associations (d) · Unintended consequences (e) · Missing data (f)
Discussion — What does it mean? (Items 14–18)
14
SummaryKey findings vs AIM (14a); project strengths (14b)
15
InterpretationIntervention-outcome association · literature comparison · impact · contextual differences · costs
16
LimitationsGeneralisability (16a) · internal validity threats (16b) · mitigation efforts (16c)
17
ConclusionsUsefulness · sustainability · spread potential · implications · next steps
18
FundingSources of funding; role of funder in design, implementation, interpretation
